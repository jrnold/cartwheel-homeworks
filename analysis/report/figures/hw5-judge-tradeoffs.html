Which judge for narration and overexplaining?

Eight judge runs, compared on how often they agree with a human Pass (TPR), how often they catch a human Fail (TNR), F1, and what each run cost. Seven runs are on the 39 dev traces. The last is the frozen v6 judge, run once on the 40 held-out test traces.

terra, prompt v0

Best balance on dev: 21 of 23 Passes and all 16 Fails. Chosen as the judge model.

luna, prompt v0

Same TPR as terra for $0.04 instead of $0.30, but it let 3 of 16 Fails through.

Jev

Under a cent per run, but it called 7 of 23 good replies failures under both question sets.

v6 on test

TPR held at 0.91. TNR fell from 0.88 on dev to 0.71 on test: 12 of 17 Fails caught.

Agreement on Passes against catching Fails

Up is fewer good replies wrongly flagged. Right is fewer failures missed. Crossbars are 95% Wilson intervals, and every dev interval overlaps, because each rate rests on 16 to 23 traces.

Jev across every threshold

Jev returns a probability of Fail for each question, and the runs above called Fail at 0.5. Sweeping that cutoff traces an ROC curve on the 39 dev traces. The six-question score is the highest of its six probabilities, which matches its "Fail if any question is at or above the cutoff" rule. Pass is the positive class, so up is more human Passes kept and right is more human Fails let through.

The 0.5 cutoff sits low on both curves, keeping 16 of 23 Passes. For the one-question version, a cutoff of 0.79 would keep 21 of 23 Passes and still catch 13 of 16 Fails, close to luna's dev result. Any cutoff picked from this chart would be tuned on dev, and Jev never ran on test, so these curves compare the two question sets; they are not a validated judge.

AUC intervals are percentile bootstraps over the 39 dev traces with 5,000 resamples. Jev reports probabilities to two decimals, so ties make some steps diagonal.
